Methods and sources

Reading the angles

A low-angle window requires the Sun above 0° and at or below 15° at the crater centre, the Moon at least 20° above the observing site’s horizon, and nautical twilight or darker. Earth must be above the crater’s horizon. Hourly samples run from 18:00 to 23:00 local time. The times listed are individual samples.

JPL Horizons gives the Moon’s topocentric altitude, subsolar and sub-observer coordinates, observer distance and twilight state. We project the USGS crater centre onto a spherical Moon, treating the Sun as distant and correcting the Earth sightline for observer distance. Libration can bring some limb features into view. A positive Earth angle near zero means a foreshortened view. Local mountains and individual shadow lengths are outside this model.
